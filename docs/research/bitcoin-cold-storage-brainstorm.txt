BITCOIN COLD STORAGE, MULTISIG BACKUPS, AIR-GAPPED SIGNING & INHERITANCE
Brainstorm — October 2026

Sources informing this document: MrHodl's thread on cold storage as a process
(x.com/mrhodl/status/2106514934570123438), Yeti 2.0 (github.com/bowlarbear/yeti-2.0),
YetiCold (github.com/JWWeatherman/yeticold), EntropyLab (oogabooga team,
github.com/entropy-lab), the collaborative custody landscape (Casa, Unchained,
Nunchuk), Liana/Miniscript timelocked recovery (Wizardsardine), SeedSigner,
and Bitcoin Core's official multisig + offline-signing tutorials.

=====================================================================
0. THE MENTAL MODEL THAT FRAMES EVERYTHING
=====================================================================

Cold storage is a process, not a device. Separate three jobs that people
conflate into one gadget:

  1. KEY GENERATION — where entropy comes from (weak entropy = everything
     downstream is theater)
  2. BACKUP — the durable recovery object (steel, paper, optical media)
  3. SIGNING — the replaceable tool you use to spend

The backup is the long-lived object; the signer is disposable. "My bitcoin is
on my hardware wallet" is the wrong sentence — the hardware wallet is just the
current signing tool. Every architecture idea below is a variation on
assigning different media, locations, and people to these three jobs.

=====================================================================
1. WHAT THE REFERENCE IMPLEMENTATIONS TEACH US
=====================================================================

YETI 2.0 (the "trusted artifact is text" model)
- Two dedicated refurbished laptops: one online running a full archival
  Bitcoin Core node, one permanently air-gapped booting a live Ubuntu USB
  with networking and swap disabled.
- Seven key wallets created on the offline machine, assembled into a
  3-of-7 wsh(sortedmulti) descriptor.
- Each key burned onto an archival DVD together with the watch-only wallet
  and the guide itself; the 7 DVDs are geographically distributed in
  non-descript envelopes.
- The scripts are intentionally identical to Bitcoin Core's official
  multisig/offline-signing tutorials — the whole thing is diffable
  line-by-line against the most scrutinized software in Bitcoin. It ships
  no software; it's a tutorial, not an application.
- Mandatory rehearsal: three test transactions exercising all 7 keys
  before real funds touch the vault.

ENTROPYLAB (the verification layer)
- Browser-based calculator: accepts dice rolls, coin flips, hex entropy,
  BIP39 seeds, xprvs, WIF keys; derives seeds, xpubs, descriptors, and
  Bitcoin Core-compatible watch-only wallet.dat files.
- Inspects PSBTs for repeated ECDSA nonces, verifies Jade anti-exfil
  transcripts, checks signatures against RFC 6979, exports BIP 388 wallet
  policies, and runs host-environment sanity checks.
- This is the "trust but verify" companion: bring your own entropy (dice),
  it proves the derivation, and it can emit a signing or watch-only Core
  wallet from the SAME root as your steel backup — the unified-backup
  trick the tweet advocates.

THE COMBINED LESSON
Entropy from dice + a verifiable calculator + Core's reference scripts +
air-gapped live-USB signing + optical/steel backups + mandatory rehearsal.
No vendor anywhere in the chain.

=====================================================================
2. VAULT ARCHITECTURE IDEAS (THE QUORUM DESIGN SPACE)
=====================================================================

A. THE CLASSIC DEEP-COLD VAULT — 3-of-7 Yeti-style
   Tolerates 4 lost keys; requires 3 compromised locations to steal. Best
   for pure savings in the $10k-$5M range. Weakness: on-chain fingerprint
   (a 3-of-7 is conspicuous), and heirs face a coordination problem.

B. TIERED CUSTODY PYRAMID — don't put everything in one vault
   - Spending (hot): phone wallet, small amounts
   - Checking (warm): 2-of-3 collaborative custody (Casa/Unchained/Nunchuk)
     — you hold majority keys, provider holds a recovery backstop and can
     never move funds unilaterally
   - Savings (cold): DIY 2-of-3 or 3-of-5 on hardware signers with Sparrow
   - Generational (deep cold): Yeti-style Core vault or timelocked
     Miniscript setup
   Different quorums, different vendors, different failure modes.
   Correlated failure (one signer bug, one compromised coordinator) can't
   take everything.

C. DECAYING MULTISIG (the elegant inheritance primitive)
   A 3-of-5 that decays to 2-of-5 after 18 months of inactivity, then
   1-of-5 after 3 years — expressed in Miniscript with relative timelocks.
   While you're alive and refreshing the timelocks, full security. After
   you're gone, the threshold drops on its own and heirs need fewer keys.
   Liana pioneered this: recovery paths enforced on-chain by Bitcoin
   itself, no third party to trust. Nunchuk 2.0 ships the same idea
   commercially — after a configurable timelock, the platform key drops
   out of the signing requirement automatically, so inheritance executes
   even if the company disappears.

D. GEOGRAPHICALLY DISTRIBUTED HETEROGENEOUS SIGNERS
   In a 3-of-5, use five DIFFERENT signer types (Coldcard, SeedSigner,
   Jade, a Core offline laptop, a Trezor) in five locations. Vendor
   diversity means no single firmware bug, supply-chain attack, or
   secure-element vulnerability is catastrophic. All major coordinators
   support mixed-device quorums.

=====================================================================
3. AIR-GAPPED SIGNING DEVICE IDEAS
=====================================================================

E. THE SEEDSIGNER MODEL AS A PHILOSOPHY
   ~$50 of generic hardware (Pi Zero 1.3 — no WiFi/BT chip, so the air gap
   is physical), stateless (keys live in RAM only, wiped on power-off),
   QR-only communication in both directions, fully open source with
   reproducible builds. Statelessness is the killer feature: a stolen or
   seized unit is a worthless bare Pi, and one device can sign for many
   cosigners.

F. QR-OVER-USB WHERE POSSIBLE
   Yeti uses USB sticks for PSBT transfer only because Core lacks QR
   signing — and explicitly notes USB is inferior to QR for limiting data
   flow, transfer frequency, and out-of-band verification. For any signer
   you design or choose: animated QR (UR/BCUR2) one-way video channel
   beats removable media.

G. THE "BURNER SIGNER" PATTERN
   A cheap laptop that boots a live USB, signs, and is wiped — no
   persistent state at all (exactly Yeti 2.0's offline machine: nothing is
   ever written to its internal drive; keys come off DVDs per-session).
   The signing environment is ephemeral; only the backup media persist.
   This inverts the hardware-wallet model — the device holds NOTHING, ever.

H. PSBT VERIFICATION AS A FIRST-CLASS RITUAL
   Before signing: decode the PSBT on a SECOND independent tool
   (EntropyLab, Sparrow, decodepsbt), confirm outputs/amounts/fee, check
   change outputs belong to your wallet, check for nonce reuse. After
   signing: re-verify before broadcast. This catches malicious coordinators
   and address-swap malware — the realistic attack on air-gapped setups.

=====================================================================
4. BACKUP & KEY-STORAGE IDEAS
=====================================================================

I. THE DESCRIPTOR IS THE FORGOTTEN BACKUP
   Everyone backs up seeds; almost nobody backs up the wallet descriptor —
   and without it, a multisig with a lost key is unrecoverable even with
   the remaining seeds, because you can't reconstruct which
   xpubs/derivations formed the quorum. Rule: every key backup location
   also gets a copy of the descriptor (privacy-sensitive, not
   spend-enabling). Yeti does this right — every DVD carries key +
   watch-wallet + guide.

J. MEDIA LAYERING PER THREAT
   - Steel (fire/flood, decades) for seeds/passphrases — crude is the
     point: no firmware, no vendor, no USB standard
   - Archival optical (DVD/M-Disc class) for wallet files + descriptors +
     recovery instructions — refresh ~every 7 years
   - Encrypted digital (AES-256-GCM JSON, PBKDF2 — the EntropyLab Journal
     model) for convenience copies
   - Paper with NATO phonetic transcription + every-5th-word checksum for
     human-readable error correction (original YetiCold trick)

K. SPLIT KNOWLEDGE, NOT JUST KEYS
   Shamir (SLIP-39) splits ONE key — fine for redundancy, but the
   reconstructed secret is still a single point of failure at use-time.
   Multisig splits SPENDING AUTHORITY and never reconstructs anything.
   Rule of thumb: Shamir for backing up a passphrase or a single-signer
   seed; multisig for the vault itself.

L. DECOY & DURESS LAYERS
   BIP39 passphrases give free hidden wallets: real vault under passphrase
   A, plausible-deniability decoy (with a believable balance) under
   passphrase B. A small decoy single-sig serves as the "give them
   something" layer under physical coercion. The $5-wrench defense is
   really DISTRIBUTION: 3-of-7 across 7 locations means no single
   location's compromise matters.

=====================================================================
5. INHERITANCE ARCHITECTURES (THE HARD PROBLEM)
=====================================================================

Core tension: heirs need access EVENTUALLY, must not have access NOW, and
you can't trust that any company, lawyer, or website exists in 30 years.

M. ON-CHAIN TIMELOCK INHERITANCE (most trustless)
   Liana's simple template: your key spends anytime; the beneficiary's key
   activates after ~15 months of wallet inactivity, enforced by Bitcoin
   consensus. Stack multiple recovery paths: family 2-of-3 after 1 year,
   professional custodian "Safety Net" after 15 months. The catch: you
   must REFRESH — send coins to yourself before each timelock expires,
   paying fees each time. A dead-man's switch where YOU wind the clock.

N. DISTRIBUTED-KEY INHERITANCE (no timelock maintenance)
   3-of-5 where you hold 3 keys, and two "inheritance keys" live with
   family/attorney/safe-deposit — useless alone today, but after death the
   heirs combine their 2 with any 1 of yours (retrieved by your executor
   per your instructions). Nunchuk's Honey Badger tiers formalize this.
   Variant: each family member holds a key that alone does nothing — no
   single heir can be targeted or coerced.

O. COLLABORATIVE CUSTODY AS INHERITANCE EXECUTOR
   Casa's 6-month uncontested claim (no KYC, recipient email); Unchained's
   document-heavy Inheritance Protocol with concierge walkthrough for
   heirs plus transfer-on-death probate bypass. The right mental model:
   you're renting a RECOVERY PROCESS FOR YOUR HEIRS, not custody for
   yourself.

P. THE "LETTER + REHEARSAL" LAYER (free, most skipped)
   Whatever the technical scheme: a sealed letter to the executor
   describing the structure (not the seeds), where pieces live, who to
   call, and which open-source tools to use — plus an actual drill. The
   most common inheritance failures are mundane: untested recovery, seed
   stored with the device, everything concentrated with one person. Write
   down the plan, not the seed; run a small dry-run with the heirs.

=====================================================================
6. OUTSIDE-THE-BOX IDEAS
=====================================================================

Q. THE "GUARDIAN QUORUM" VAULT
   2-of-4: you, spouse, a family friend who doesn't know what the envelope
   contains, a safety-deposit box. Any two — but combinations designed so
   no pair existing today (besides you+spouse) can collude without
   triggering notice.

R. ANTI-EXFIL BY CONSTRUCTION
   Signers supporting sign-to-contract / anti-exfil protocols (Jade,
   Coldcard) make the signature itself prove the nonce wasn't covertly
   chosen to leak the key — verify transcripts independently with
   EntropyLab. Paranoid tier: dice-generated entropy; never let a device's
   RNG near your seed at all.

S. THE VERIFIABLE-TUTORIAL SIGNING KIT (Yeti philosophy, productized)
   A live-USB image whose ENTIRE signing logic is a short shell script
   diffable against Bitcoin Core's docs, booted on any generic laptop,
   networking hard-disabled, keys loaded from optical media, PSBTs in/out
   on a one-way QR bridge. The security claim isn't "trust us" — it's
   "diff 40 lines."

T. PROGRESSIVE DECENTRALIZATION OF YOUR OWN CUSTODY
   Year 1: collaborative custody 2-of-3 while you learn (you can always
   exit — Casa publishes sovereign-recovery docs, Unchained open-sourced
   Caravan as the escape hatch). Year 2: migrate to DIY 3-of-5 with
   diverse signers. Year 3: add timelocked decaying paths for inheritance.
   Each stage, you shed a trust assumption only after rehearsing the next.

U. MULTISIG-AS-CANARY
   A tiny "tripwire" UTXO in a separate single-sig whose seed is stored in
   your house. If someone physically searches and finds it, they'll sweep
   the decoy — and the on-chain movement is your intrusion alarm (watch it
   with your own node over Tor).

V. MUSIG2/TAPROOT WHEN IT MATURES
   Taproot multisig makes the quorum indistinguishable from single-sig
   on-chain — fixing Yeti's known privacy leak (a visible 3-of-7
   fingerprints you). Watch Nunchuk's MuSig2 beta; don't build the deep
   vault on it until signing-device support broadens.

=====================================================================
7. FAILURE-MODE CHECKLIST (WHAT ACTUALLY KILLS SETUPS)
=====================================================================

  1. Lost descriptor        -> multisig unrecoverable despite holding
                               seeds (back it up everywhere)
  2. Untested recovery      -> rehearse with real small transactions
                               before funding (Yeti mandates 3 test spends
                               covering all 7 keys)
  3. Seed + device co-located -> geographic separation is the entire point
  4. Timelock lapse         -> calendar reminders + fee budget for
                               refreshes; overlapping recovery paths as
                               backup
  5. Single-vendor monoculture -> mix signer hardware and coordinator
                               software
  6. Trusted setup help     -> Yeti's whole design goal is that a
                               moderately technical person follows it
                               ALONE, because a helper is an attack vector
  7. PSBT signed blind      -> verify before and after, on a second tool

=====================================================================
8. THREE CONCRETE REFERENCE STACKS
=====================================================================

MINIMAL (~$100)
  Vault:        2-of-3 Sparrow multisig
  Signers:      2x SeedSigner (DIY) + 1 mobile key
  Backup:       steel x3 + descriptor x3
  Inheritance:  letter + key with attorney

ROBUST (~$500)
  Vault:        Liana decaying 2-of-3 -> 1-of-3 @ 15 months
  Signers:      Coldcard Q + Jade + SeedSigner
  Backup:       steel + encrypted JSON + descriptor in 3 locations
  Inheritance:  timelocked heir key (trustless) + rehearsal

PARANOID (~$750, Yeti-class)
  Vault:        Core 3-of-7, full archival node, Tor
  Signers:      air-gapped live-USB laptop
  Backup:       7 archival DVDs (key+descriptor+guide each), 7 locations
  Inheritance:  distributed envelopes + executor letter; optionally wrap
                in Liana-style timelocks later

=====================================================================
THE UNIFYING PRINCIPLE
=====================================================================

Distribute trust across media, locations, vendors, people, and time — and
make every recovery path one you've actually walked through at least once
with real (small) money.
